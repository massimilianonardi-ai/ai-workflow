# Research that reaches a useful conclusion

Use for research, factual investigation, options studies and exploration informing a task. Research is not a separate mutually exclusive route; combine it with design, diagnosis or review as appropriate.

1. **Define the decision or knowledge product.** Record the concrete question, target audience, period/geography/version, requested depth, why it matters and the form of the result. Distinguish exploration from a requested exhaustive review.
2. **Decompose into answerable claims.** Identify known constraints, competing hypotheses, critical unknowns and what evidence could distinguish them. Favor primary/authoritative material; cross-check when uncertainty could change the decision.
3. **Gather proportionately and with provenance.** Verify current versions, dates and scope when material. Record source identity, what it actually establishes and limitations. A search result, summary or quote is not independent confirmation of applicability.
4. **Synthesize rather than accumulate.** Contrast material alternatives, surface contradictory evidence, distinguish fact from inference and state confidence/remaining unknowns. Follow leads only when they can change the answer or user's next decision.
5. **Deliver the decision-ready output.** Answer the original question, explain the basis and make a bounded recommendation if requested. If a critical fact is inaccessible, identify it and continue unaffected conclusions; do not invent certainty.
6. **Stop when the requested knowledge outcome is sufficiently supported.** No endless literature collection, unrelated experiments or automatic new testing agenda.

For research leading into implementation, open operator-level architecture decisions in behavior/decisions.md and maintain the same task's specification; do not silently turn a research hypothesis into an approved design.
